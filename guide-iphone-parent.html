Set-up guide

Your child's iPhone, set up from your iPhone or iPad

Only a few apps will work, like calls, texts and maps. Everything else is blocked. You can change the settings later from your own device.

Before you start

You'll need:

  • your own iPhone or iPad, and your child's iPhone, both charged
  • your child's Apple Account set up in your Family Sharing group (step 1 checks this)
  • a new 4-digit code that your child does not know. This is your "Screen Time passcode". Don't use your phone's unlock code or a birthday.
  • about 30 minutes when your child is around, so you can test it together at the end
It's a good idea to tell your child what you're doing and why before you start. See how other parents have explained it.

Steps

  1. Check your child is in your Family group

    On your iPhone or iPad:

    1. Open Settings.
    2. Tap your name at the top, then tap Family.
    3. Check your child's name is in the list.

    If your child is not there, tap Add Member and follow the instructions. Help with Family Sharing

    Watch this step (1 minute)

  2. Turn on Screen Time for your child

    1. Go back to Settings and tap Screen Time.
    2. Under Family, tap your child's name.
    3. Tap Turn On Screen Time, then Continue.

    If you see questions about age-appropriate content, you can accept the suggestions. We'll change the settings that matter in the next steps.

    Watch this step (1 minute)

  3. Set your Screen Time passcode

    1. On your child's Screen Time page, tap Use Screen Time Passcode.
    2. Type in your new 4-digit code twice.
    3. If asked, enter your Apple Account password so you can reset the code if you forget it.
    Write the code down somewhere your child won't find it. Without it, they cannot change any of the settings you're about to make.
    Watch this step (1 minute)

  4. Block apps all day with Downtime

    Updated 21 September 2026

    In iOS 26.1, Every Day is now inside Scheduled. If you set up before this date, you don't need to do anything.

    1. On your child's Screen Time page, tap Downtime.
    2. Turn on Scheduled, then choose Every Day.
    3. Set From to 00:00 and To to 23:59.
    4. Turn on Block at Downtime.

    This blocks every app, all day, apart from the ones you allow in the next step.

    Watch this step (2 minutes)

  5. Choose the apps your child can use

    1. Go back and tap Always Allowed.
    2. Make sure these are in the Allowed Apps list. Tap the green + to add any that are missing:
      • Phone, Messages, Maps
      • Camera, Photos, Clock, Calculator
      • your child's school app [TBC]
    3. Remove anything else from the list by tapping the red −.
    Watch this step (2 minutes)

  6. Stop new apps being installed

    1. Go back and tap Content & Privacy Restrictions. Turn it on.
    2. Tap App Store, Media, Web & Games, then App Store Purchases (or iTunes & App Store Purchases).
    3. Set Installing Apps, Deleting Apps and In-app Purchases to Don't Allow.
    Watch this step (1 minute)

  7. Stop the settings being changed

    1. Still in Content & Privacy Restrictions, scroll down to Allow Changes.
    2. Set Passcode Changes and Account Changes to Don't Allow.
    Watch this step (1 minute)

  8. Only allow approved websites

    1. In Content & Privacy Restrictions, tap App Store, Media, Web & Games, then Web Content.
    2. Choose Only Approved Websites.
    3. Tap Add Website and add your child's school website.
    Watch this step (1 minute)

  9. Check it works, together

    On your child's iPhone:

    1. Try to open an app that should be blocked, like YouTube or a game. You should see a Downtime screen.
    2. Open Messages or Phone. These should work as normal.
    3. Try to open the App Store. It should be hidden or blocked.

    If something doesn't work as expected, tell us.

Pictures of each step. These phone screens are drawn in code (HTML), not screenshots. That's deliberate for the mock-up, but it's also an option for the real thing:

  • Real screenshots look exactly like the parent's phone, but need re-taking after every iOS update, and a test device with no personal data on it.
  • Drawn screens (like these) are updated by editing text, can be read by screen readers, and still show the parent exactly what to tap (red box). They look less "real".
  • We could mix them: drawn screens in the web guide and real screenshots in the video.
